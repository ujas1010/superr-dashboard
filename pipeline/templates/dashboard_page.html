Pressure = how much one ball can change who wins

A win-probability model learned from the data (out-of-sample AUC — in chases, — in first innings) estimates each side's chance of winning before every ball. A ball's leverage is the expected swing in that chance across its possible outcomes. The Pressure Index divides leverage by its average, so 1.0 is a typical ball. It combines required rate, wickets, balls left and how close the game is, and it scores the batter and the bowler on the same moment.

          Method in brief
          • Data: the supplied IPL 2022-26 and T20I ball-by-ball pack. Excluded: two A-team events, no-result matches and Super Overs.
          • Every ball is compared with what an average player would produce in the same situation (over, wickets, chase equation, competition, team strength, venue, pace or spin). Wickets are converted to runs with a run-expectancy model.
          • Rating = 50% pressure level + 25% change from the player's own ordinary-situation level + 25% consistency, as z-scores. Each component is shrunk for sample size, so noisy parts carry little weight.
          • Eligibility: at least 120 high-pressure balls in at least 12 matches, and 150 ordinary balls.
          • The full pipeline (Python) rebuilds every number here; the rating code and parameters are attached to each source.
